Section 4

Meet Dr. Reed — Ask Me Anything

Open live to camera here: who you are, your background, and why you teach this course the way you do. Then move into the scripted Q&A slides that follow.
"I've never coded before — am I going to be okay in this class?"
Yes. This course is built assuming zero prior experience, and most students here have never written a line of code before Module 1. Do the reading before lab, run your code often instead of writing twenty lines and hoping, and come to office hours with a specific error instead of "I'm stuck."
A specific error takes five minutes to work through. "I'm stuck" takes much longer to untangle. Encourage students to bring code + error message, not vague descriptions.
"What's the best way to reach you, and how fast will you respond?"
Canvas Mail or your USF email — not a DM, not a discussion comment. 48 business-hour response window on weekdays; Friday-evening emails get answered by end of day Monday. Always include your U Number and section number.
Emphasize: no weekend replies, and incomplete emails (missing U Number/section) take longer to resolve.
"What if I'm behind, or I think I'm going to fail?"
Talk to me before it happens, not after. The one 48-hour extension exists for exactly this. The earlier you tell me, the more room I have to work with — I can't retroactively fix a missed deadline after the fact with no communication beforehand.
This is not favoritism — it's the only way the late policy can work fairly for everyone.
"What's your grading philosophy?"
I grade what you actually submit against a clear, published rubric — not effort you told me about but didn't show in the code. One week from when a grade posts in Canvas to raise a dispute; after that, it's final.
Point to the Lab Grading Rubric on the syllabus page: 4 pts correctness, 3 pts completion, 2 pts code quality, 1 pt submission.
"Office hours — when, and what should I bring?"
Monday and Wednesday, 1:30–3:00 PM, in CIS 2070B. Can't make it in person? Check Canvas for the Teams link. Bring your code, the full error message or traceback, and a one-sentence description of what you already tried.
Repeat the room number and days out loud — this is the single most-forgotten detail from Day One.
