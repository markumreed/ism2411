Capstone — Business Data Analysis

A single sustained project on a messy, real-shaped business dataset of about 5,000 rows. You pick the dataset that fits your major or the field you want to work in, clean it, ask three to five business questions, answer them with code and visualizations, and explain your findings in a 5-minute recorded video. This is the deliverable you will add to your portfolio.

Module 16 Unit 5 Capstone — 25% of grade
What this is: A practical demonstration that you can take a CSV file no one has explained to you, figure out what it contains, decide what is interesting, and tell that story clearly with code and charts. This is the exact skill employers are paying for when they hire an analyst straight out of a business program.
Don’t forget: you also record a 5-minute video

Choose your dataset

Pick the dataset that fits your major, or the field you want to work in. All eight are fictional, have 5,000 rows each, and are messy in the same ways, so every choice is graded on the same rubric. Choose one by the Module 13 milestone, download it into your repo’s data folder, and stay with it to the end. The walkthrough video above uses the Retail dataset; every step works the same on the others.

Every dataset is messy in the same ways
  • Column names with spaces and capitals (Order Date)
  • Dates that won’t parse: a few typed another way (03/14/2025) or junk like TBD, plus some blanks
  • Money stored as text ("$1,299.00"), with negatives written "-$45.00"
  • Negative amounts that mean something: your card says what
  • Typos in one category column: wrong capitals, extra spaces, and two misspellings that .str.strip().str.title() alone won’t fix
  • Blank cells in one text column and one number column
  • Exact duplicate rows
  • A few impossible values that only describe() reveals

For the typos, clean the case and spaces first, then look at what’s left and map each misspelling to the right name:

df["product_category"] = df["product_category"].str.strip().str.title()
print(df["product_category"].value_counts())    # spot the misspellings
df["product_category"] = df["product_category"].replace({"Electronix": "Electronics"})

Cleaning it well — and documenting your decisions — is half the assignment. A messy dataset that produces wrong statistics is worse than no dataset at all. Column names below are shown after you standardize them.

General Business · Entrepreneurship · Undecided

Retail Store Sales

Which products make the money, and which ones keep coming back?

One row per order line at a home-and-office retailer, 6 columns. The video and the code skeleton below use this one.

Columns
  • order_date — date of the sale
  • product_category — one of 8 categories
  • region — North, South, East, or West
  • unit_price — price per item; negative = a return
  • quantity_sold — units in the order
  • customer_segment — Consumer, Corporate, or Home Office
Starter questions
  • Which category drives the most revenue, and is it the same in every region?
  • Which categories get returned most, and what do returns cost?
  • Do Corporate customers place bigger orders than Consumers?

Accounting

Accounts Payable Ledger

Where is the money going, and who gets paid late, or twice?

One row per vendor invoice at a mid-size company, 8 columns. Here a duplicate row is an invoice entered twice.

Columns
  • invoice_number — the vendor’s invoice number
  • invoice_date — date of the invoice
  • vendor — who sent it (16 vendors)
  • expense_category — Travel, Software, Office Supplies, Utilities, Consulting, Marketing, Equipment, or Meals
  • department — the department the expense belongs to
  • amount — invoice total; negative = a credit memo (the vendor owes us)
  • payment_terms — Net 15, Net 30, or Net 60 (days allowed to pay)
  • days_to_pay — days from invoice to payment
Starter questions
  • Which vendors and terms get paid late most often? (Late means days_to_pay is more than the terms allow.)
  • Which departments spend the most, and on what?
  • Does spending jump at the start or end of the year?

Finance · Economics

Small-Business Loan Portfolio

Which loans pay off, and which ones blow up?

One row per small-business loan at a Florida community bank, 11 columns.

Columns
  • loan_id — the loan’s ID

  • region — Tampa Bay, Central Florida, South Florida, or North Florida

  • loan_status — Current, Paid Off, Late, or Default
  • profit — what the bank has earned so far; negative = money lost on a default
Starter questions
  • How does the default rate change as credit scores fall?
  • Which industries make the bank money, and which lose it?
  • Do riskier borrowers pay enough extra interest to cover the losses?

Complete code skeleton

This is the structural template for your capstone script. Fill in the analysis sections with your own business questions and chart code. Do not change the section structure — the grader follows it.

The CLEAN section is a worked example on the Retail dataset. If you chose another dataset, keep every step and swap in your own columns: your card’s column list tells you which ones hold the date, the money, the negatives, and the category with typos.

"""
ISM2411 Capstone — [Your Dataset] Analysis
Author: [Your Name]
Date:   [Submission Date]

Business Questions:
  1. [Your question 1]
  2. [Your question 2]
  3. [Your question 3]

AI Disclosure: [What, if anything, you used AI for]
"""

import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns

# ── 1. LOAD ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATA_FILE = "data/capstone_retail.csv"   # ← your dataset's file
df = pd.read_csv(DATA_FILE)
print("Raw shape:", df.shape)
print(df.head())
df.info()
print(df.isnull().sum())

# ── 2. CLEAN ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
# Document every decision with a comment explaining WHY.

# Standardize column names
df.columns = df.columns.str.lower().str.replace(" ", "_")

# Drop rows missing essential identifiers
# Reason: cannot attribute revenue without an order date
df = df.dropna(subset=["order_date"])

# Parse order_date — coerce bad dates to NaT
df["order_date"] = pd.to_datetime(df["order_date"], errors="coerce")

# Fix unit_price: strip "$" and ",", convert to float
df["unit_price"] = df["unit_price"].str.replace("$", "", regex=False)
df["unit_price"] = df["unit_price"].str.replace(",", "", regex=False).astype(float)

# Exclude returns (negative unit_price)
# Reason: question focuses on forward sales; returns analyzed separately
df = df[df["unit_price"] >= 0]

# Remove impossible values that describe() reveals
# Reason: a quantity of 9999 is a placeholder someone typed, not a real order
df = df[df["quantity_sold"] != 9999]

# Fill missing quantity_sold with median (low %, aggregate impact minimal)
df["quantity_sold"] = df["quantity_sold"].fillna(df["quantity_sold"].median())

# Fix category typos: case and spaces first, then the misspellings
# that value_counts() shows
df["product_category"] = df["product_category"].str.strip().str.title()
df["product_category"] = df["product_category"].replace(
    {"Electronix": "Electronics", "Furnitu Re": "Furniture"}
)

# Remove duplicates
df = df.drop_duplicates()
print("Clean shape:", df.shape)

# ── 3. DERIVED COLUMNS ────────────────────────────────────────
df["revenue"] = df["unit_price"] * df["quantity_sold"]
df["month"] = df["order_date"].dt.month
df["quarter"] = df["order_date"].dt.quarter

# ── 4. DESCRIPTIVE SUMMARY ───────────────────────────────────
print("\nDescriptive stats (clean):")
print(df.describe())
print("\nRevenue by region:")
print(df.groupby("region")["revenue"].sum().sort_values(ascending=False))

# [Your chart code here — with title, axis labels, savefig, show]
# [Write a one-sentence finding in a comment]

# [Analysis + chart]

# ── 7. BUSINESS QUESTION 3 ────────────────────────────────────
# [State your question]

Example business questions

You choose 3–5. Your dataset’s card has starter questions; pick from those or invent your own (better questions earn higher scores). The examples below use the Retail columns, but the same kinds of question work on every dataset: which group leads and is it the same everywhere, what the seasonal pattern is, how two segments differ, where the negatives cluster, and how concentrated the top tier is.

  • Which product category drove the most revenue, and was that consistent across all four regions?
  • Is there a seasonal pattern in sales? Which months are strongest, and by how much above the annual average?
  • How does the Corporate customer segment differ from Consumer in average order size?
  • Are there product categories with a disproportionately high share of returns (negative unit_price rows)?
  • Which region has the highest average order value, and what is driving it — more orders or higher per-order spend?
  • Did revenue grow or decline quarter over quarter? Which quarter had the steepest change?
  • What share of total revenue comes from orders above the 90th percentile? (the "top tier" concentration question)
What "strong" looks like
  • Every cleaning step has a comment explaining the business reason for the decision
  • Every chart has a title, axis labels, and is saved as a PNG (not just displayed)
  • Findings are stated as numbers with context: "Region X drove $Y, which is Z% of total" — never "X seemed to do well"
  • The code runs clean from top to bottom on a fresh download of your repo — no hardcoded paths, no missing imports
  • The README tells a reader how to run your code in 3–5 lines, including which packages to install
  • The video is specific and confident: one finding, one chart, one business recommendation, in about 5 minutes
What "weak" looks like
  • Unlabeled charts produced by df.plot() with no formatting — no title, no axis labels
  • Findings stated as opinions: "I think Electronics is popular" or "revenue seems high in the South"
  • Code that crashes or produces errors because cleaning was incomplete
  • A README that just says "run the file" with no context about requirements or execution
  • Three "questions" that are the same question rephrased three ways
  • plt.savefig() called after plt.show(), producing blank PNG files
  • No video link in the README, or a Private video the grader can’t open

Chart labeling checklist

Every chart in your capstone must pass this checklist before submission:

  • plt.title("Descriptive Title") — tells the reader what they are looking at
  • plt.xlabel("What this axis measures") — with units if applicable
  • plt.ylabel("What this axis measures") — with units if applicable
  • plt.tight_layout() — prevents clipped labels
  • plt.savefig("charts/chartname.png", dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight") — saved BEFORE show()
  • A one-sentence written interpretation below the chart code (as a comment or markdown cell)

Video guide

Your video replaces an in-class presentation. Record it at home, with your face and your screen both visible the whole time. Aim for 5 minutes; anywhere from 4 to 6 is fine, and the grader stops watching at 7:00. You do not need slides: share your screen and walk through your script or notebook and your charts. Structure it as follows:

  1. One sentence of context (30 seconds): "I analyzed about 5,000 [orders, invoices, loans, tickets…] from the [your dataset] data. After cleaning, I had X rows across Y [categories, departments, carriers…]."
  2. Your most interesting finding (about 3 minutes): State the question. Show the chart. Read the key number. Explain what it means for the business. What should leadership do differently as a result?
  3. One other finding (about 1 minute): A short version of the same structure.
  4. One thing you would do next (30 seconds): If you had more time or more data, what analysis would you run?
